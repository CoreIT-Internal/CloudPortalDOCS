# Single sign-on (SSO)

With SSO, your users sign in to CloudPortal with their existing organization account. For **Microsoft Entra ID** there is a ready-made connection in three steps. Other providers are connected as an **OpenID Connect** provider.

You configure SSO under **Settings** → **Access** → **Auth & SSO**. This requires the **Organization administration** permission.

::note
Are you an MSP partner configuring this for a customer? The same Entra connection and **Require SSO** are available under **Partner → Organizations → [customer] → Auth & SSO**, if you are allowed to administer the customer.
::

## Signing in never creates accounts

SSO only decides **how** a person signs in, not **who** gets in. Anyone signing in via SSO must already have an account and be an active member of the organization, for example after an [invitation](/en/organization/members). Anyone else sees:

> You have been authenticated, but no access context could be determined. Contact your administrator.

If you want accounts to be created automatically from your identity provider, use [SCIM provisioning](/en/organization/scim).

## Single sign-on with Microsoft Entra ID

The **Single sign-on with Microsoft Entra ID** panel guides you through three steps: prove the domain, let an Entra administrator approve the connection, then sign in through it once so SSO can be required. You do not need to create an app registration or manage a client secret.

### Step 1: Verify your domain

1. Enter your email domain (for example `company.com`) and select **Add domain**.
2. Create the TXT record the panel shows in the DNS zone for the domain:
   - **Record name:** `_cloudportal-verify.<your-domain>`
   - **Record value:** the verification string the panel shows
3. The portal checks for the record automatically, and the status changes from **Awaiting DNS** to **Verified**. You don't need to come back and click anything. To check right away, select **Check now**.

When the domain is done, it says "The TXT record has proved the domain is yours."

::tip
If the domain's DNS zone is already managed in CloudPortal, the **Create the TXT record for me** button is shown. The portal then creates the record, and the domain is verified automatically once the record is visible in DNS.
::

DNS changes can take a while to spread. If the record isn't found yet, you see "The TXT record was not found yet. DNS changes can take a while to spread."

With **Remove** you remove a domain. For a verified domain, the **Remove {domain}?** box warns that users with an email address on the domain no longer find the organization's sign-in. If the organization requires SSO and it is the last verified domain, the change is refused.

### Step 2: Connect Microsoft Entra ID

1. Select **Connect Microsoft Entra ID**. The button is active once the domain is verified.
2. You are sent to Microsoft. There an Entra administrator approves the connection for the whole organization and signs in to complete it. Nothing is activated until that is done.

The approval is given, and the connection completed, by an Entra administrator holding one of **Global Administrator**, **Privileged Role Administrator**, **Cloud Application Administrator** or **Application Administrator**. Custom roles do not count, and a PIM role must be activated. If you don't have such a role yourself, do the step together with someone who does, in the same browser you are signed in to the portal with.

When the connection is completed you return to the portal and the connection is active. If the administrator's sign-in can be linked to an account in the organization, they are signed in straight away. Otherwise they land on the sign-in page, but the connection is still active.

::note
If you open the page on the organization's [own domain](/en/organization/custom-domain), you see instead "The SSO connection is activated at the portal's main address. Microsoft returns there, and the activation completes only for a sign-in there." Use the **Open this page at {host}** link and do the step there.
::

::note
Looking a domain up in Entra only shows which Entra tenant it points at. It is not proof of ownership. The TXT record in step 1 is the proof.
::

### Step 3: Status

The status box shows:

- **Connection** — **Connected** or **Not connected**.
- **Entra tenant** — the ID of the connected Entra tenant.
- **First sign-in** — **Completed** once someone has signed in through the connection, otherwise **Not yet**. **Changed since** means the connection was changed after the last successful sign-in.

Sign in once via Microsoft to confirm that the connection works. This is required before you can require SSO.

### If something goes wrong

| Message | What to do |
|---|---|
| "The approval was declined in Microsoft. Nothing was changed." | Ask an Entra administrator with the right role to do step 2 again. |
| "The person who completed the connection is not an administrator in the Microsoft tenant. …" | Do step 2 again and sign in to Microsoft with an account holding one of the roles above. A PIM role must be activated. |
| "Microsoft refused the request (code …)." | Try again, or send the code to support. |

## Require SSO (optional)

When **Require SSO** is ticked, all users must sign in via your identity provider. The box can only be ticked when:

- a domain is verified
- a connection is active, either Microsoft Entra ID or another identity provider (see **Advanced** below)
- at least one sign-in through an active connection has succeeded (for Entra, **First sign-in** then shows **Completed**)

If something is missing, the portal shows what remains. If the connection has changed since it was last used, it says "The connection has changed (issuer, tenant or client) since it last carried a sign-in. Requiring SSO is blocked until someone signs in through it again."

Select **Save changes** when you're done.

**Allow local login for owners** keeps password sign-in as a fallback for owners, even when SSO is required. We recommend leaving it ticked.

::warning
If you turn off **Allow local login for owners** while SSO is required, you must tick **I confirm there is another administrative way in**. If SSO stops working you could otherwise be locked out of the portal. The confirmation is not stored; you are asked again the next time you make such a change.
::

## Require MFA (optional)

With **Require MFA**, "everyone who signs in with a password and reaches the organization" must have two-step verification, the provider's staff included. Anyone without it sets it up at the next sign-in. A sign-in through SSO counts as met. Select **Save changes**.

## Advanced: other identity providers

If you don't use Microsoft Entra ID, you can connect an OpenID Connect provider by hand, for example OneLogin. Most organizations do not need this. Open **Advanced: other identity providers** on the **Auth & SSO** page. At least one domain must be verified (step 1 above) before you can add a provider.

1. Select **Add IdP**. The provider is **OpenID Connect**.
2. Enter **Key (URL-safe slug)**, a short ID such as `oidc`, and an optional **Display name (optional)**. The display name appears on the sign-in button when there are several ways to sign in.
3. Fill in the details from your identity provider:
   - **Issuer (Discovery URL)**, for example `https://idp.example.com`
   - **Metadata URL (optional)** — otherwise the issuer's standard address is used
   - **Client ID**
   - **Client Secret**
   - **Scopes (comma-separated)** — optional, the default is `openid,email,profile`
4. The dialog shows **Redirect URI (enter it in your IdP):**. Register it as an allowed redirect/callback with your identity provider:
   ```
   https://<portal-address>/api/auth/sso/<slug>/callback
   ```
   `<slug>` is the organization's slug, shown at the top of the **Auth & SSO** page.
5. Leave **Enabled** ticked and select **Create**. With **Sort order** you control the order when there are several providers. The status shows **Active** when the configuration is valid.

::tip
The Redirect URI always points at the portal's main address, even when you open **Auth & SSO** from a [custom domain](/en/organization/custom-domain). Sign-in goes through that address, so that is the one you register.
::

Then sign in once through the provider. That confirms the connection works, and it is required before you can require SSO (see **Require SSO** above).

You turn a provider off with its toggle. The portal asks **Turn off "{name}"?**. If the organization requires SSO and the provider is the last tested way in, the change is refused. If you delete a provider, no one can sign in through it any more, and this cannot be undone.

## Custom domain and SSO

If you use a [custom domain](/en/organization/custom-domain), SSO works there too. The sign-in passes the portal's main address and then returns to your domain. For Microsoft Entra ID you don't need to register any extra redirect URI. For an OpenID Connect provider, **one** redirect URI is enough (see above).

The connection to Microsoft Entra ID itself is always activated at the portal's main address (see step 2).

::note
Signing out of the portal does not sign you out of Microsoft. A new Microsoft sign-in may therefore go through without asking for the password.
::
