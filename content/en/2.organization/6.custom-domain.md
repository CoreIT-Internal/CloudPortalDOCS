# Custom domain

You can let your organization reach the portal on its **own domain**, for example `portal.company.com`, instead of the standard address. The domain gets an SSL certificate automatically, and your users can sign in directly on it. You configure it on the **Custom domain** page under **Settings** → **Appearance** → **Domain**.

::note
If you are a provider or distributor, **Domain** in the partner area applies to your own tenant. A customer's domain is set in the customer's own settings.
::

## Entering the domain

1. Go to **Settings** → **Appearance** → **Domain**.
2. Enter the domain under **Custom domain**, without `https://` (e.g. `portal.company.com`), and click **Save**.
3. The portal now shows the DNS records to add with your DNS provider, with copy buttons.

## Step 1: Point your domain

Create a **CNAME** record that points the traffic to the portal:

| Type | Name | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `CNAME` | `portal.company.com` (your domain) | `portal.coreit.network` |

Keep the record for as long as the domain is in use.

## Step 2: Verify ownership

Create a **TXT** record that shows you own the domain:

| Type | Name | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `TXT` | `_cloudportal-verify.portal.company.com` | `cp-verify=…` (the value the portal shows) |

Once the record exists, click **Verify domain**. The portal also checks automatically at regular intervals. It can take up to 24 hours for DNS changes to propagate.

::note
The domain is reserved for you for a while. The portal shows for example "The domain is reserved for you until {date}. If it is not verified by then it is released so that others can use it, and a new reservation gets a new TXT record."
::

## Waiting for activation

Once the domain is verified, the **Domain protection & certificate** box shows how far it has come: **Provisioning queued → Waiting for certificate → Active**. It also shows "Last synchronized: {date}". When the status is **Active**, the domain is live with a valid SSL certificate.

::warning
DNS changes can take time to take effect. If the status becomes **Moved**, the CNAME record no longer points correctly; **Error** shows a reason. Check the records against what the portal shows.
::

## Signing in on the domain

The **Signing in on the domain** card shows what applies when someone signs in on your domain:

- **Ownership verified** — the TXT record proves the domain is yours.
- **Technically ready** — the certificate is active and the domain points at the portal.
- **Sign-in available** — the sign-in page shows your brand, and sign-ins can stay on the domain.

If something is missing under **Technically ready**, it says what, for example "The domain does not point at the portal. Check the CNAME record above." or "The DNS check has not run yet. It runs automatically, normally within five minutes."

Under **Who signs in here** it says who can stay on the domain: the organization's own users, when the account reaches no other organization. "Everyone else is signed in at {host} instead, and told why." That applies for example to the provider's staff and to someone who is also a member of another organization.

For your users this means:

- The sign-in page on the domain shows your [branding](/en/organization/branding).
- If the account has access that requires the portal's main address, the sign-in page shows "Your account has access that requires signing in at the portal's main address." with the **Sign in at the main address** button.
- If signing in on the domain is not available right now, the user is signed in at the portal's main address and told so.
- Signing in with SSO works on the domain. It passes the portal's main address and then returns. See [SSO](/en/organization/sso#custom-domain-and-sso).

## Replacing or removing the domain

- **Remove** — the portal asks **Remove {domain}?**. Sign-in through the domain stops working at once, and the organization uses the standard address instead.
- If you save a new domain while the old one is verified, the portal asks **Replace the sign-in domain?**. The old domain stops working at once, and the new one has to be verified before it can be used.
