# DNS zones

A DNS zone corresponds to a domain (for example `example.com`) and holds all the DNS records for that domain. The zone list shows the zones linked to your organization, and from it you open a zone to work with its records.

## View your zones

1. Go to **Domains** → **DNS**. The **Zones** page opens with the **DNS zones** list.
2. The list has the columns **Zone**, **Status** and **Records**. Under the zone name you see which server the zone is on. When the number of records is not known, "–" is shown.
3. Use the **Search zones** field to filter by name.
4. Use the sort menu to sort by **Name A–Z**, **Name Z–A**, **Most records** or **Fewest records**.
5. Click **Refresh zones** (the circular arrow) to load the list again.

### Status

| Status | Means |
|---|---|
| **Delegated** | The domain points to CoreIT's name servers. |
| **Pending delegation** | The domain does not point, or not only, to CoreIT's name servers. The row shows which ones are missing. Change the name servers at your domain registrar. |
| **Shared access** | The zone is owned by another organization and shared with yours. See [Shared access](/en/domains-and-dns/shared-access). |

::warning
When you change name servers at the registrar it can take up to 24 hours before the change takes effect (propagation), often faster.
::

### How zones get into the list

The first time the organization opens the zone list with no zones in it, the portal looks up the zones the organization can reach and activates them. It shows how many were found, for example "3 zone(s) found and activated automatically".

After that, new zones appear in the list on their own: zones the organization becomes owner of and zones shared with yours, as long as they are not hidden. You control which zones are shown or hidden with [Manage zones](/en/domains-and-dns/manage-zones).

### If no zones are shown

The list shows **No zones found**, and what you can do:

- If you are allowed to create zones, the portal suggests creating the first one with **Create Zone**.
- Otherwise it says "The organization has no DNS zones. Ask an administrator to create one."

## Create a zone

You need the **Create DNS zones** permission.

1. Click **Create Zone** in the zone list. The **Create DNS Zone** panel opens.
2. Enter the domain name under **Domain**.
3. The panel shows which name servers the domain should point to (**Change name servers at your registrar to:**), with a copy button for each.
4. Click **Create Zone**. When the zone has been created, "Zone … has been created." and the **Open Zone** button are shown.
5. Change the name servers at your registrar. Until they point correctly the zone shows **Pending delegation**.

## Domain names with international characters

Domain names can contain characters such as å, ä and ö, for example `kurtåkerbåt.com`. In DNS such names are written in an ASCII form that starts with `xn--`.

- **When typing:** you can type the name with the ordinary characters when creating a zone and in record names and targets. Under the field you see how the name is written in DNS, for example "In DNS: xn--…".
- **When reading:** the portal shows names with the ordinary characters. Hover over a name to see the ASCII form. In the zone header, **Show the DNS name** shows the form, and on a record there is **Copy DNS name**, which always copies the ASCII form.
- **When searching:** searches match both the ordinary form and the ASCII form.

::note
Top-level domains with international characters (for example `.рф`) are not supported yet.
::

## Open a zone

Click a zone in the list to open it. The zone header shows the zone's status and the buttons:

- **Manage Redirects** — the zone's redirects, see [Redirects](/en/domains-and-dns/redirects). The button is only shown to the zone's owner.
- **Changes** — [Recent changes](/en/domains-and-dns/recent-changes) for this zone only.
- **Export** and **Import** — see below.

Below the header you find:

- **Records** — the workspace where you create and change DNS records. See [DNS records](/en/domains-and-dns/dns-records).
- **Zone details** — a collapsible box with the zone's name servers and the zone information (SOA). Both are managed automatically and cannot be changed in the portal.
- **Shared access** — shown when the zone is shared. See [Shared access](/en/domains-and-dns/shared-access).

## Export and import zone files

- **Export a zone:** click **Export** on the zone page, or choose **Export zone file** in the zone's menu in the list. You get a text file with all records, useful as a backup. Requires **Export DNS zones**.
- **Export several zones:** click **Export zones** in the zone list (the button is shown when there is more than one zone) and choose which. If you choose many, they are downloaded as a ZIP file.
- **Import:** click **Import** on the zone page and choose a zone file. The preview shows what happens to each record under the tabs **Will import**, **Skipped** and **Errors** before you confirm. Requires **Import DNS records** and **Edit DNS records**.

::note
Some records are never imported because they are managed automatically, including the SOA record, the zone's name servers and DNSSEC records.
::

## Delete a zone

You need the **Edit DNS zones** permission, and the organization must own the zone. A zone shared with yours can only be deleted by its owner.

1. At the bottom of the zone page you find **Delete DNS zone**. Click **Delete zone**.
2. Type the zone's name to confirm. Both the ordinary form and the ASCII form are accepted.

You can also delete several zones at once in [Manage zones](/en/domains-and-dns/manage-zones).

::warning
Deleting a zone permanently removes the zone and all its DNS records. It cannot be undone.
::

## Cloudflare zones

Zones in the organization's own Cloudflare account are managed in the **Cloudflare DNS** module under **Domains**. The interface works largely the same way: a zone list, zone pages with records, **Search records** and **Recent changes**.

Differences to be aware of:

- **Connection:** the module requires that the organization has connected its Cloudflare account. Otherwise "Cloudflare is not connected" is shown. The connection is made by someone with the **Manage the Cloudflare connection** permission.
- **Waiting for name servers:** a new zone has this status until the domain points to Cloudflare's name servers. The zone page shows which name servers the domain should point to.
- **Proxied or DNS only:** a proxied record sends traffic through Cloudflare. **DNS only** means Cloudflare only answers DNS queries. Proxied records always have automatic TTL.
