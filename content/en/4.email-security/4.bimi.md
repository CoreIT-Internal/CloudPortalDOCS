# BIMI

BIMI (Brand Indicators for Message Identification) is a DNS record that points recipients to your logo and, if you have one, your Mark Certificate. Some recipients, such as Gmail, Yahoo and Apple, can then display the logo next to authenticated email.

The portal checks the logo, the certificate and DMARC, can host the files for you, and writes the BIMI record at `default._bimi.<domain>`.

::warning
Publishing the BIMI record is necessary but not sufficient. Recipients require DMARC in enforcement and often a Mark Certificate before the logo is shown. "Display is decided by the receiving mailbox provider and cannot be guaranteed by the portal."
::

## Prerequisites

- **The organization owns the domain in the portal.** The same rule as for MTA-STS: automatically when the domain's zone in **DNS** is delegated to our name servers, otherwise with a TXT record. See [Domain ownership and transfer](/en/email-security/domain-ownership-and-transfer).
- **DMARC in enforcement.** All of these requirements must be met:
  - The policy that governs the domain is `p=quarantine` with `pct=100`, or `p=reject`.
  - The organizational domain's own `p` is not `none`, also when a subdomain has a record of its own.
  - A published `sp` for subdomains is not `none`.
- **A logo in SVG Tiny PS**, the SVG profile that BIMI requires, reachable over HTTPS.
- **A Mark Certificate** (VMC or CMC) in PEM format. It is optional, but many recipients require it.

DMARC and the certificate do not stop publishing. If they are not met, the logo is not expected to show.

## Create a BIMI policy

1. Go to **Email security** → **BIMI** and click **New BIMI policy**. From a zone you can instead click **BIMI for this domain**. If there is no policy, click **Create a BIMI policy for …**, and the domain is filled in.
2. Type the domain under **Domain**. The portal checks whether there already is a BIMI policy or a BIMI record in DNS for the domain:
   - "A BIMI policy already exists for this domain." — click **Open existing policy**.
   - "An existing BIMI TXT record was found in DNS." — when you publish, you approve the portal taking over the record. See [Publish](#publish).
   - If another organization owns the domain, "The domain is already managed by another organization in the portal" is shown, with the link **Request a transfer**.
3. Under **Brand logo (required)**, choose:
   - **Upload (portal hosts it)** — choose an SVG file.
   - **External URL** — enter an HTTPS address of an SVG you host yourself.
4. Under **Mark Certificate (optional, recommended)**, choose **No certificate**, **Upload (portal hosts it)** or **External URL**.
5. Click **Create draft**.

The policy is created as a draft, and the record is not published until you choose to. If an upload was refused, "The policy was created, but the upload failed: …" is shown. Upload a corrected file on the policy page.

## Logo and certificate

### The logo

An uploaded logo is checked against SVG Tiny PS. If it is not, it is not uploaded: "The logo is not SVG Tiny PS, which BIMI requires, and was not uploaded." Below the message the problems are listed with line numbers, for example that:

- `<svg>` lacks `version="1.2"` and `baseProfile="tiny-ps"`
- `<svg>` has `x` or `y`, which Illustrator adds
- `<title>` is missing. It must be the first element in `<svg>` and hold the brand name.

Fix the problems and upload the file again. What the profile only recommends is listed under "The profile recommends:", for example at most 32 KB and a `<title>` of at most 64 characters. Such a file is uploaded. The file may be at most 128 KiB.

The policy page shows the logo the record points at, "as a receiver may show it".

### The certificate

An uploaded certificate is tested as a VMC for the domain and the record's selector. If it is not accepted, it is not uploaded: "The certificate is not accepted as a VMC for the domain and was not uploaded." The reason is shown below, for example that the file only holds an ordinary TLS certificate, that the chain is broken, that a certificate has expired or that it does not cover the domain.

The check has limits: "The portal does not check that the chain ends in a BIMI root the receiver trusts, nor revocation, nor that the logo in the certificate is the one published. The receiver does."

::warning
Only upload the public certificate file. Never upload a private key.
::

### Hosted files

Uploaded files are hosted on the environment's BIMI host, a domain of its own, separate from the portal's.

- An uploaded file takes precedence over a URL. The URL is stored, but not used as long as the file is there.
- Uploading or replacing a file does not change DNS. Publish again for the record to point at the new file.
- A file cannot be removed while the policy is published, because the record may point at it. Upload a replacement with **Replace** and publish, or unpublish first.

You change external URLs under **BIMI configuration** and save with **Save**. A URL must start with `https://` and must not contain a semicolon or whitespace.

## Run verification

Click **Run verification**. The portal checks:

- that the logo's and the certificate's URLs can be fetched
- that the logo is SVG Tiny PS
- that the certificate is a VMC for the domain
- that DMARC meets BIMI's requirement
- that the record in the zone matches the policy.

The results are listed under **Latest verification checks** as **Passed**, **Warning**, **Failed** or **Unknown**. A logo or certificate at an external URL that is not accepted gets the same explanation as an upload.

If DMARC does not meet the requirement, the box "DMARC does not meet BIMI's requirement" is shown, with the requirements that are not met, for example "The organizational domain's DMARC has p=none. Then no logo is shown, not for a subdomain either."

## Phase and markers

At the top of the policy page is the policy's phase and the next step. The same phase is shown in the list, which can be filtered by **Domain** and **Phase**.

| Phase | Means |
| --- | --- |
| **Ownership not verified** | Verify ownership of the domain. |
| **Owned by another organization** | Another organization owns the domain. Request a transfer if it belongs here. |
| **Transferred** | The domain has been transferred. The policy is read-only. |
| **Logo missing** | Upload the logo or give its URL. |
| **Draft** | The record is not published. Publish it. |
| **Publishing** | The record is being written. |
| **Retrying** | A step was refused and is retried automatically. |
| **Waiting for approval** | A record in the zone that is not the portal's is in the way. Approve the takeover. |
| **Held** | Publishing is waiting for an action. The reason is on the policy page. |
| **Outcome being settled** | The outcome of a write is being settled. The page reads itself again. |
| **Waiting for the customer's DNS** | Add the TXT record where the domain's DNS is managed. |
| **Published in DNS** | The record is in DNS. |
| **Failed** | Publishing failed. Publish again. |

Beside the phase is what the latest verification found for **DMARC**, **the logo**, **the certificate** and, for a published policy, **the record in the zone**. For example "The logo is SVG Tiny PS." or "No certificate. Many receivers require a VMC or CMC." Below the markers it says "Display is decided by the receiving mailbox provider and cannot be guaranteed by the portal."

## Publish

1. Click **Publish**. The dialog **Publish the BIMI record?** shows the record that is written, where it is written and the markers.
2. Click **Publish**.

Where the record is written:

| The domain's DNS | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| A zone in **DNS** or **Cloudflare DNS**, and you may change records there | The portal writes the record in the zone. |
| A zone in the portal, but you may not change records there | The policy is published as customer-managed. The record is added by hand. |
| No zone in the portal | The portal does not write the record, it shows it. Add it where the domain's DNS is managed. |

For customer-managed DNS the phase is **Waiting for the customer's DNS** until the record is in place.

If there already is a BIMI record in the zone that the portal did not create, publishing stops and the phase is **Waiting for approval**. Click **Review and take over**, tick "I approve removing exactly these records." and click **Take over the records**. It works as for MTA-STS, see [Records in the zone that are not the portal's](/en/email-security/mta-sts#records-in-the-zone-that-are-not-the-portals). Taking over a record requires the right to edit DNS records in the zone.

If the record in the zone has changed after publishing, "DNS record drifted from the policy. Click Publish to restore." is shown.

## Unpublish or delete

- **Unpublish** removes the BIMI record from DNS and keeps the policy. BIMI has no decommissioning like MTA-STS: the record is removed at once.
- **Delete** removes the policy. A published policy is unpublished first. Deleting a published policy removes its DNS record, which requires **Publish email security policies**.

Unpublishing works even when editing is frozen.

## Check the result

Use the [Lookup tool](/en/email-security/lookup-tool) to check that the BIMI record exists at `default._bimi.<domain>` and that DMARC looks right.
