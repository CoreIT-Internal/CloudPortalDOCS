# Domain ownership and transfer

A domain can be owned by only one organization in the whole portal. Ownership applies to both MTA-STS and BIMI. Only the organization that owns the domain can create, change and publish policies for it.

Ownership is proven with DNS. Whoever controls the domain's DNS can always take the domain over through a transfer.

## Prove ownership

There are two ways to prove that the organization owns the domain:

- **Delegation to our nameservers** — the organization has the domain's zone in **DNS**, and the zone is publicly delegated to our name servers. The portal checks this automatically.
- **TXT record** — you add a TXT record at `_email-security-verify.<domain>` with a value the portal shows.

A zone in **Cloudflare DNS**, or a zone that another organization shares with you, is not proof enough. Use the TXT record instead.

The **Domain ownership** box on the policy page shows the status: **Unverified**, **Auto-trusted** (proven by delegation), **Verified** or **Transferred**.

### Verify with a TXT record

1. Open the policy. The **Domain ownership** box shows **Record name (TXT)** and **Record value**, with copy buttons.
2. Add the record where the domain's DNS is managed.
3. Click **Verify now**.

The portal asks two public resolvers. If the record is not visible, it says why, for example "No matching TXT record was found." or "The public resolvers see different answers — the record is still propagating. Try again in a few minutes."

The instructions are shown only to those who have **Manage email security policies**.

## The domain owner

When the organization owns the domain, the **Domain owner** box shows for example "Your organization has owned the domain in the portal since …", and how ownership was proven: **TXT record** or **Delegation to our nameservers**.

### Keep the proof in place

"Keep the TXT record in the domain's DNS. Ownership is checked regularly, and if the record is consistently missing for at least a day, editing is frozen until it is back."

The same applies to the delegation. Ownership does not change if the zone later moves, but the delegation is checked regularly.

### When control cannot be confirmed

If the domain no longer exists in public DNS, or the proof has gone, the **Domain owner** box first shows "Control of the domain could not be confirmed". It is checked again in twelve hours. Editing is frozen only once this has been seen consistently from two public resolvers for at least a day.

Then it says "Editing is frozen":

- nothing can be published or changed for the domain
- what is published keeps being served
- you can still decommission an MTA-STS policy and unpublish a BIMI policy.

The organization's owners and administrators, and administrators at your provider, get an email.

To lift the freeze:

1. Put the TXT record, or the delegation, back.
2. Click **Check now**. If the proof is visible, it says "Ownership is confirmed again." Otherwise it says "The proof is still not visible. DNS changes can take a while to show; try again in a few minutes."

If the domain is no longer yours: decommission and unpublish its policies and release the domain.

### Release the domain

With **Release the domain**, the organization stops owning the domain in the portal. Another organization can then take it with a proof of its own. Your policies for the domain become unverified, but nothing published changes.

The button is shown only when no policy for the domain is served. Decommission or unpublish first.

## When another organization owns the domain

If you try to create a policy for a domain that another organization owns, "The domain is already managed by another organization in the portal" is shown. Which organization it is is never shown. If you control the domain's DNS, you can request a transfer with **Request a transfer**.

## Request a transfer

The **Domain transfer** page describes the steps under **How it works**:

1. Click **Request a transfer**. You get a TXT record unique to your organization, with **Record name (TXT)** and **Record value**.
2. Add the record to the domain's DNS and click **Check**. When the record is visible it says "The record is visible. The current owner has been notified, and the transfer can be completed at … at the earliest."
3. Wait 72 hours. The record must stay in place the whole time.
4. Click **Complete the transfer** and confirm. The record is checked again. If it is still there, ownership moves to your organization, and the previous owner's policies become read-only.

With **Withdraw** you cancel the request. A request that is not completed within 14 days expires. The page shows when, under "Expires …".

| State | Means |
| --- | --- |
| **Requested — waiting for the TXT record** | Add the record and click **Check**. |
| **Proven — waiting time** | The record is visible. Wait until the transfer can be completed. |
| **Completed** | Your organization owns the domain. |
| **Failed** | The transfer did not go through, for example because the record was gone. |
| **Expired** | The request was not completed within 14 days. |
| **Cancelled** | The request was cancelled, for example because you withdrew it. |

Requesting and completing a transfer requires **Manage email security policies**.

## When someone requests a transfer of your domain

When another organization has proven its TXT record, the **Domain owner** box shows "Another organization has requested a transfer", and when the transfer can be completed at the earliest. The organization's owners and administrators get an email. They also get an email when the transfer is completed, fails or is withdrawn.

Whoever controls the domain's DNS decides. If the request is unexpected: remove the `_email-security-verify` record from the domain's DNS and contact your support. Without the record, the transfer does not go through.

## After a transfer

**For the new owner:** the previous owner's published MTA-STS policy is served unchanged, as inherited, until you publish your own. You can also click **Stop serving the inherited policy**. No MTA-STS policy is then served for the domain until you publish your own. Senders that already have the policy cached keep using it until it expires. This requires **Publish email security policies**.

**For the previous owner:** the policies for the domain get the phase **Transferred** and are read-only. A policy that is served as inherited has the phase **Inherited** and cannot be deleted while it is served. With **Request a transfer back** you can ask for the domain back the same way.
