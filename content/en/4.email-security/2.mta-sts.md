# MTA-STS

MTA-STS lets you require other mail servers to use TLS (encryption) when they deliver email to your domain, and to deliver only to your real MX hosts. It protects against eavesdropping and misdirected delivery if the DNS answer is tampered with.

The portal hosts the policy file over HTTPS at `mta-sts.<domain>` and writes the DNS records that are needed, so you don't have to run your own web server.

## Testing and enforce

A policy has a mode:

- **Testing** — sending mail servers report errors but still deliver. Use it to roll the policy out.
- **Enforce** — sending mail servers refuse to deliver to an MX that is not in the policy, or whose TLS fails.

Senders keep the policy in their cache for up to **max_age** seconds. So a change is not seen by every sender at once, and a policy in enforce cannot be taken back in one go.

A new policy is always published in testing first. Once it has been served in testing long enough, you switch to enforce after a precheck. See [Enforce and MX change](/en/email-security/enforce-and-mx-change).

## Prerequisites

- **The organization owns the domain in the portal.** Ownership is proven automatically when the organization has the domain's zone in **DNS** and the zone is delegated to our name servers. Otherwise you add a TXT record at `_email-security-verify.<domain>`. A zone in **Cloudflare DNS** is not proof enough. See [Domain ownership and transfer](/en/email-security/domain-ownership-and-transfer).
- **At least one MX host** to include in the policy, usually the same hosts as your MX records.
- **An address for TLS-RPT reports** (`mailto:` or `https:`). It is optional in testing but required for enforce.

## Create a policy

1. Go to **Email security** → **MTA-STS** and click **New policy**. From a zone you can instead click **MTA-STS for this domain**. If there is no policy, click **Create a policy for the domain**, and the domain is filled in.
2. Type the domain under **Domain**. The organization's DNS zones are suggested in the field. Below the field it says "Ownership is proven automatically when the domain is delegated to our name servers. Otherwise the portal shows a TXT record to add."
3. Under **MTA-STS mode**, **Testing** is selected. **Enforce** cannot be chosen for a new policy: "A new policy is published in testing first. Enforce can be chosen once it has been served in testing long enough."
4. Add your hosts under **MX hosts** (for example `mx1.example.com`). A pattern may start with `*.`, for example `*.mail.example.com`.
5. Enter **Max age (seconds)**, how long senders may keep the policy. The default is 86400 (one day).
6. If needed, tick **Enable TLS-RPT reporting** and enter the address.
7. Click **Create draft**. The policy is saved but not published.

Once you have typed the domain, the portal checks whether there already is a policy or MTA-STS records for it:

- **A policy already exists:** click **Open existing policy**.
- **Another organization owns the domain:** the box "The domain is already managed by another organization in the portal" is shown, with the link **Request a transfer**. See [Domain ownership and transfer](/en/email-security/domain-ownership-and-transfer).
- **There already are MTA-STS records:** the box "Existing MTA-STS records found" lists them, and the **Origin** column says whether a record was created by the portal or outside it. The button **Create and take over existing records** is then also shown. It creates the policy and publishes it at once. Records the portal did not create are still not removed until you have approved it on the policy page, see [Records in the zone that are not the portal's](#records-in-the-zone-that-are-not-the-portals).

## The policy page

Click a policy in the list to open it. The list can be filtered by **Domain** and **Phase**.

At the top is the policy's **phase** and the next step (see [Phases](#phases)). Beside the phase, the ownership status is shown: **Unverified**, **Auto-trusted**, **Verified** or **Transferred**. The badge **Out of sync** is shown when a published record in the zone is missing or has changed.

The buttons in the header are **Verify**, **Publish**, **Decommission** and **Delete**:

- **Verify** checks the records and the policy file again.
- **Publish** is called **Publish changes** when the draft has changes, and **Publish again** when it does not.
- **Decommission** is shown when the policy is served.

A button you can never use with your role is not shown. A button that cannot be used right now is grey, and the reason is written below the buttons, for example "Decommission the policy before deleting it." On a narrow screen one main button is shown, and the rest are under **More actions**.

Further down the page you find:

- **Domain ownership** and **Domain owner** — see [Domain ownership and transfer](/en/email-security/domain-ownership-and-transfer).
- **Draft** (**Policy** before the policy has been published) — the policy's settings, which you change here.
- **DNS records** — the records the policy needs, with a status for each record.
- **Policy hosting** — the state of the hosting of the policy file.
- **Served now** and **Draft** — the policy file that is served and, if the draft differs, the draft's text beside it.

## Change the policy

"Changes are saved as a draft. What is served changes only when you publish."

1. Change the mode, MX hosts, **max_age** or TLS-RPT in the draft. For **max_age** there are the presets **1 day**, **1 week**, **2 weeks** and **1 year**.
2. Click **Save draft**. **Undo the changes** takes you back to the saved draft.
3. The box "There are changes that are not published" shows which fields differ from what is served.
4. Publish when you are done.

If someone else saved the draft while you were changing it, the form keeps your changes, and you choose **Keep my changes** or **Use the saved one**.

If you switch the mode to **Enforce**, the portal suggests a **max_age** of one week, and the draft must pass a precheck before it is published. See [Enforce and MX change](/en/email-security/enforce-and-mx-change).

## Publish

1. Click **Publish** (or **Publish changes** or **Publish again**).
2. The dialog shows exactly what is published: **Mode**, **MX hosts**, **max_age** and **TLS-RPT**. A value that changes is highlighted, with the old value as "was …". In enforce it also says how long senders keep the policy.
3. When the text changes it says "The policy gets a new id, so senders that cached the previous one fetch the new one."
4. Review the records under **Records to apply** and click **Publish**.

If the draft changed while the dialog was open, the new draft is shown and you confirm again.

### Who writes the records

Above the **DNS records** table it says who writes the records:

| Situation | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| The domain's zone is in **DNS** or **Cloudflare DNS**, and you may change records there | "The portal writes the records in the zone … when the policy is published, and keeps them current. You do not need to add them yourself." |
| The zone is in the portal, but you may not change records there | The policy is published as customer-managed. The records are added by hand, or by someone with the right to edit DNS records. |
| The domain is not a zone in the portal | You add the records where the domain's DNS is managed. The portal checks them afterwards. |
| The zone is shared with you but owned by another organization | The portal writes no records in it. Add the records yourself, or ask the zone's owner. |

A customer-managed policy is served, and the box **Published as customer-managed** says why. The phase is **Waiting for the customer's DNS** until the records are in place.

The table shows a status per record: **OK**, **Mismatch**, **Missing**, **Unknown** or **Written on publish**. The last one means the portal writes the record when you publish.

### The order it happens in

Senders that see the TXT record fetch the policy file right away. So the file must be fetchable before the TXT record exists. Publishing happens in this order:

1. The CNAME record `mta-sts.<domain>` is written. It points at the portal's hosting.
2. The portal waits for the hostname and its certificate. The phase is **Waiting for certificate**, and **Policy hosting** shows **Preparing SSL…**. It usually takes a few minutes, up to 15 minutes.
3. The policy file is served. The TXT records `_mta-sts.<domain>` and, with TLS-RPT, `_smtp._tls.<domain>` are written last.
4. The phase is **Served, not confirmed in DNS** until the public resolvers show the policy's id. Then the phase is **Live**.

For customer-managed DNS only the CNAME record is shown at first: "Add this record where the domain's DNS is managed. The certificate is issued once it points here, and the TXT records are shown once the policy is served:"

While publishing is in progress the page reads itself again. What was already published is served as before until the new one is ready.

### Records in the zone that are not the portal's

If there are records in the zone that the portal did not create, and that are in the way of the policy's records, publishing stops before anything changes. The phase is **Waiting for approval**, and the box **Records in the zone that are not the portal's** lists the records.

1. Click **Review and take over**. The dialog **Take over the DNS records** shows exactly the records that are removed.
2. Tick "I approve removing exactly these records."
3. Click **Take over the records**.

The records are replaced by the policy's records, and their content is kept in the log. If the records in the zone change before the publish runs, the approval no longer holds and you are asked again.

Taking over records requires **Publish email security policies** and the right to edit DNS records in the zone.

### Limit on the number of policies

The organization can have a limited number of MTA-STS hosts. When the limit is reached, publishing a new policy stops before anything changes, and the phase is **Held**. Decommission a policy that is no longer needed and publish again.

## Phases

Each policy has a phase, and the same phase is shown in the list and on the policy page. Below the phase is the next step.

| Phase | Means |
| --- | --- |
| **Draft** | The policy is not published. Publish it. |
| **Ownership not verified** | The organization has not proven that it owns the domain. |
| **Owned by another organization** | Another organization owns the domain. Request a transfer if it belongs here. |
| **Inherited** | The domain has been transferred, and the policy is served for the new owner until it publishes its own. |
| **Transferred** | The domain has been transferred. The policy is read-only. |
| **Waiting for certificate** | The hostname and certificate are being set up. For customer-managed DNS: add the CNAME record. |
| **Publishing** | The records are being written. Nothing to do. |
| **Retrying** | A step was refused and is retried automatically. The reason is on the policy page. |
| **Waiting for approval** | Records in the zone that are not the portal's are in the way. Approve the portal taking them over. |
| **Held** | Publishing is waiting for an action. The reason is on the policy page. |
| **Outcome being settled** | The answer to a write was lost. The portal reads the record back before it does anything more. |
| **Waiting for the customer's DNS** | The policy is served. Add the TXT record where the domain's DNS is managed. |
| **Served, not confirmed in DNS** | The policy is served. The portal is waiting for the policy's id to show at the public resolvers. |
| **Live** | The policy is served, and its id shows in DNS. |
| **Failed** | Publishing was stopped. Fix the cause and publish again. |
| **Decommissioning** | A policy with `mode: none` is served until cached policies have expired. |
| **DNS points here** | The policy is no longer served, but the domain's DNS still points here. Remove the records. |
| **Decommissioned** | Nothing is served. The policy can be deleted. |

## Policy hosting

The **Policy hosting** box shows the state of the hosting of the policy file at `mta-sts.<domain>`:

| Status | Means |
| --- | --- |
| **Not active** | Hosting is activated when you publish. |
| **Activating** | Activation has been requested. It usually takes a few minutes. |
| **Preparing SSL…** | The certificate is being prepared. It can take up to 15 minutes. |
| **Active** | The policy file is served over HTTPS. |
| **DNS link missing** | The CNAME record no longer points at the hosting. Restore it, otherwise hosting is removed after 7 days. |
| **Removed** | Hosting has been removed. Publish again to activate it. |
| **Error** | Activation failed. The portal retries. Contact support if the error persists. |

## When something changes outside the portal

- **The records have been changed or removed in DNS:** the policy gets the badge **Out of sync**, and the box "DNS records are out of sync with the policy" is shown. Click **Publish again** to restore the records, or decommission the policy if it is no longer used.
- **The domain's MX points at hosts that a policy in enforce does not cover:** see [Enforce and MX change](/en/email-security/enforce-and-mx-change#mx-monitoring-in-enforce).
- **Editing is frozen:** control of the domain could not be confirmed. See [Domain ownership and transfer](/en/email-security/domain-ownership-and-transfer#when-control-cannot-be-confirmed).

## Decommission a policy

Senders that have the policy in their cache keep using it for up to **max_age** seconds, even if the records are removed. If the policy is in enforce, they may then refuse to deliver if MX changes. So a policy is not removed at once, but decommissioned:

1. The portal serves a policy with `mode: none` under a new id. Senders that fetch it stop applying the previous policy.
2. The hosting and the DNS records are removed only once no sender can still hold a protective policy in its cache. It takes about as long as the policy's **max_age**. With a **max_age** of one week, it takes a little over a week.

Here is how:

1. Click **Decommission**. The dialog **Decommission MTA-STS for …** says how many days it takes.
2. Click **Decommission**. The phase becomes **Decommissioning**.
3. Until then you can publish again. When everything is removed, the phase is **Decommissioned**.

If the domain's DNS still has records pointing at the portal after the policy has been decommissioned, for example with customer-managed DNS, the phase becomes **DNS points here**. Remove the records where the domain's DNS is managed. The name stays reserved until they are gone.

::warning
Tick **Remove now instead** only if you must. The hosting and the DNS records are then removed now, and you confirm by typing the domain's name. A sender that holds the policy in its cache keeps applying it for up to **max_age**. If the policy is in enforce, mail may be delayed or bounce if MX changes in that time.
::

Decommissioning requires **Publish email security policies**. It works even when editing is frozen.

## Delete a policy

A policy that is served must be decommissioned first. When the phase is **Decommissioned**, or if the policy was never published, click **Delete** and confirm.

- Deleting a policy requires **Manage email security policies**. If DNS records or a hostname remain that are removed with the policy, **Publish email security policies** is also required.
- A policy that is served as inherited for a new owner cannot be deleted while it is served.

## Check the result

Use the [Lookup tool](/en/email-security/lookup-tool) to check that the TXT record, the policy file and the TLS-RPT record are visible publicly, and that the policy covers the domain's MX.
