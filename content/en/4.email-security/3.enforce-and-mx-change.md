# Enforce and MX change

In enforce, sending mail servers refuse to deliver to an MX that the MTA-STS policy does not name, or whose TLS fails. They also keep the policy for up to **max_age** seconds. A mistake in enforce can therefore stop mail to the domain, and it cannot be fixed in one go.

That is why the portal helps you in three ways:

- a **precheck** before a policy is published in enforce
- **monitoring of the domain's MX** when a policy is published in enforce
- an **MX change in four steps**, so that senders always have a policy that covers the MX they deliver to.

How to create and publish a policy is described in [MTA-STS](/en/email-security/mta-sts).

## Switch to enforce

A new policy is always published in testing. Let it be served in testing for a while and read the TLS-RPT reports before you switch.

1. Open the policy. Choose **Enforce** in the draft. The portal then suggests a **max_age** of one week, if it was lower.
2. Click **Save draft**. The box **Precheck for enforce** is shown.
3. Click **Check**. The portal tests the draft against four requirements (see the table below).
4. Read the outcome:
   - **Ready for enforce** — you can publish.
   - **Ready once the MX below are acknowledged** — the portal did not reach every MX. Tick the box for each MX: "I know the portal could not check STARTTLS on …, and I publish anyway".
   - **Not everything could be checked. Check again** — for example if the public resolvers did not give the same answer. Try again in a moment.
   - **Not ready for enforce** — something stops it. Fix it and check again.
5. Click **Publish**. The dialog reminds you that senders refuse to deliver to an MX that is not in the list, and how long they keep the policy.

| Requirement | What the portal checks |
| --- | --- |
| **TLS-RPT** | That TLS-RPT is turned on with an address. It is the only way to hear of failed deliveries. |
| **Time in testing** | That the policy has been served in testing, with its id visible in DNS, without a break for at least the number of days the box shows (normally 7). |
| **MX covered** | That both public resolvers give the same MX for the domain, and that every MX is covered by a pattern in the draft. |
| **STARTTLS per MX** | That every MX offers STARTTLS on port 25 with a certificate valid for its name. The portal checks at most 10 MX. |

Each requirement is shown as **met**, **stops**, **could not be checked** or **needs acknowledging**, with what the portal found.

A check is good for 30 minutes, and only for the draft as it was then. If you change the draft it says "The draft has changed since the check. Check again."

::note
"Time in testing alone does not prove that enforce is safe, and the portal does not read the TLS-RPT reports." Read the reports yourself before you switch modes.
::

The precheck is shown to those who have **Publish email security policies**. No precheck is needed to go back from enforce to testing, or to publish again when the draft has no changes.

## MX monitoring in enforce

When a policy is published in enforce, the portal monitors the domain's MX. If MX points at a host that the policy does not name, senders that honor the policy would refuse to deliver there.

- **MX the policy may not cover** — the portal has seen it, but not confirmed it. It is checked again in 15 minutes, and confirmed only once it has been seen consistently from two public resolvers for at least 30 minutes.
- **MX the policy does not cover** — confirmed. Mail to those hosts may not arrive.

"The policy has not been changed: the portal never weakens a policy by itself." Instead, the portal prepares a draft with the hosts added, if the policy does not already have a draft. The organization's owners and administrators, and administrators at your provider, get an email.

Do this:

1. Open the policy and review the draft. If the policy already had a draft, add the hosts to it yourself.
2. Run the precheck and publish, or move the MX records back if the change was a mistake.

## Change MX in enforce

In enforce you cannot just change the MX records. Senders that have the old policy in their cache would refuse to deliver to the new MX. The box **Change MX** guides you through four steps:

1. **Add the new patterns** — the draft names both the old and the new patterns, and you publish it.
2. **Wait** — until senders can have the new policy.
3. **Switch MX in DNS** — you switch MX where the domain's DNS is managed.
4. **Remove the old patterns** — when it is safe, you publish the draft without the old patterns.

The box is shown for a policy published in enforce. In testing no steps are needed: change MX and update the policy's MX hosts as usual.

### Start the change

1. Publish or remove the draft's changes first. A draft saved identical to what is published does not count as a change.
2. Click **Change MX…**. The list is filled in with the published patterns.
3. Enter the patterns that should apply after the change: add the new ones and remove those that should go.
4. Choose **Level**:
   - **Normal** — waits until the policy's new id is seen in DNS and the previous record's TTL has passed. A sender that still holds an older policy may delay mail to the new MX for a while, but should fetch the new policy before the mail bounces.
   - **Conservative** — also waits until every older policy without the new patterns has expired. It can take as long as the longest **max_age** ever published.
5. Click **Start the MX change**. The draft now names both sets.

### Follow the steps

Each step is shown as **done**, **now**, **later** or **not needed**, and below the steps it says what to do next and from when.

1. Click **Publish step 1**. Publishing goes through the usual dialog and the precheck.
2. Wait until the time shown. Don't switch MX before then.
3. Switch MX where the domain's DNS is managed. The portal checks every 15 minutes and sees the switch when both public resolvers show MX that only the new patterns cover. Keep the old MX servers accepting mail.
4. When it says the old patterns can be removed, click **Prepare the removal**. The draft then no longer names the old patterns. Click **Publish step 4**.

If you switch MX before it is safe, it says "MX was switched before it was safe. A sender with an older policy may have delayed mail to the new MX." If you try to publish a draft that removes the old patterns too early, the portal refuses.

With **Cancel the MX change** the portal stops following the steps. What is published and the draft stay as they are.

Changing MX in steps requires **Publish email security policies**.
